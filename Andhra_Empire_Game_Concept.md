# ANDHRA EMPIRE — ఆంధ్ర సామ్రాజ్యం

**Tagline:** *Ooru Needhe. Business Needhe. Rajyam Needhe!*

**Genre:** Andhra Pradesh–inspired property trading, negotiation, and regional business strategy  
**Status:** Concept / first-playtest specification  
**Audience:** Families, Telugu-speaking players, and casual tabletop groups  
**Core promise:** Buy, build, trade, and grow a business empire across Andhra Pradesh—while festivals, tourism, trade, and unpredictable events change your plans.

> **Important:** All purchase prices, rents, taxes, fees, and rewards are fictional **in-game values**, not market real-estate prices. This is an original game concept inspired by classic property-trading games; commercial artwork, brand identity, rules, and packaging should be independently designed and legally reviewed before release.

---

## 1. Game vision

Players begin as local entrepreneurs with **₹15 lakh** in game money. They travel around a 40-space circuit representing Uttarandhra, Coastal Andhra, and Rayalaseema; buy commercial opportunities in recognizable locations; develop shops and landmark businesses; trade with one another; and adapt to regional economic events.

**What gives it an Andhra identity:**

- The three regional identities—**Uttarandhra, Coastal Andhra, and Rayalaseema**—all appear on the board.
- The economy includes **agriculture, crafts, tourism, coastal trade, transport, and commerce**.
- Two original event decks introduce local humor and surprises: **Mana Adrushtam** and **Oori Kaburlu**.
- Seasonal mechanics reference **Sankranti**, tourist demand, market conditions, and trade contracts.
- Cards and graphics use **Telugu and English**, with modern folk-art visual language.

**Design objective:** Easy to learn for families, with optional deeper strategy for repeat players.

**Starting play:** Each player chooses a token, receives ₹15 lakh, and places it on **Andhra Yatra** (Start). Shuffle both event decks separately and place them face down. Choose a banker and roll to decide first player.

**In-game scoring after 12 rounds:** Cash + purchase value of owned properties and hubs/utilities + money spent on retained upgrades − outstanding debts. Highest total wins. Players can alternatively choose an elimination-based long game.

## 6. Property ownership and building upgrades

**Base property rent:** Approximately 10% of the buy price, shown in the table above.

**Complete-collection bonus:** If the owner has all three properties in a color collection, charge **1.5× base rent** for unimproved properties in that collection.

**Business-development ladder:**

1. **Level 0 — Vacant business opportunity:** Charge base rent (or the collection bonus if completed).
2. **Level 1 — Local shop:** Charge **2× base rent**.
3. **Level 2 — Commercial complex:** Charge **3× base rent**.
4. **Level 3 — Landmark business:** Charge **5× base rent**.

A player may upgrade only once they own the entire three-property collection. Each property may have up to three upgrade markers. An event effect overrides normal rent only when its card says so.

### Example property card: Rajamahendravaram

**Collection:** Godavari Gold  
**Purchase price:** ₹2,20,000  
**Base rent:** ₹22,000  
**Complete collection, no upgrades:** ₹33,000  
**Level 1 — Local shop:** ₹44,000 rent

**Card description:** *A commercial opportunity in the iconic Godavari river region.*

> Proposed economy balance: Start with upgrade costs around 20–25% of property price per level, rounded to playable denominations. Confirm the final prices through multiplayer simulations and real playtests.

## 7. Special Andhra mechanics

### A. Sankranti Business Boom

Every **third complete round**, trigger a festival phase. Draw or reveal a festival modifier that increases revenue for one eligible category: agriculture, regional travel, tourism, or crafts. Festival bonuses expire after the announced round. Festival tokens may be used as single-use discounts or benefits, depending on the chosen event cards.

### B. Mirchi Market

Players may make small investments in a shared fictional chilli market. Event cards move the market up or down; an investor earns or loses money when they cash out. Set a per-player investment cap to avoid runaway advantages.

### C. Andhra Crafts Collection

Create bonus cards for **Kondapalli toys, Kalamkari, Uppada textiles, Bobbili Veena, and Etikoppaka toys**. Completing a three-card craft objective unlocks a one-time payout or property upgrade discount. Use these as commercial inspiration, not as claims of exclusive ownership of cultural heritage.

### D. Coastal Trade

Players with eligible coastal properties can buy limited-duration export contracts. Successful contracts provide additional income; delays or demand changes reduce revenue. Use no more than one active contract per player in the basic advanced rules.

### E. Tourism Season

Araku Valley, Lepakshi, Gandikota, and other tourism-linked businesses receive bonuses during tourism events. This should provide a counterweight to expensive high-tier commerce properties.

**Recommendation:** Basic Mode uses only the event decks, property sets, and collection bonuses. Advanced Mode unlocks Mirchi Market, contracts, craft objectives, and festival planning.

## 11. Visual and illustration direction

**Primary look:** Modern Telugu folk art, inspired by Andhra's craft traditions rather than a direct copy of conventional Monopoly designs.

- **Typography:** Big, legible English titles with a strong Telugu companion font, such as Noto Sans Telugu.
- **Palette:** Deep indigo, terracotta red, turmeric yellow, river green, coastal blue, and antique cream.
- **Board centerpiece:** An illustrated Andhra Pradesh silhouette with the Krishna and Godavari rivers, the Visakhapatnam coast, Konaseema coconut groves, Rayalaseema forts, and Amaravati skyline.
- **Illustration language:** Fine Kalamkari-inspired decorative borders alongside vivid, playful Kondapalli-inspired figurative illustrations.
- **Property cards:** Color-coded header, large bilingual title, distinctive illustrated scene, purchase price, rent ladder, and upgrade rules.
- **Events:** Bold hand-lettered titles, an expressive local character, one-sentence situation, and a highly visible effect area.
- **Usability:** Large currency values, minimal small print, consistent symbols, and quick icon recognition.

**Suggested board format:** Approximately **50 × 50 cm** when unfolded, pending manufacturing samples and legibility tests.

## 14. Rules, balancing, and preproduction checklist

### Core turn sequence

1. **Roll and move** clockwise (unless an event changes movement).
2. **Resolve the destination:** Purchase an unowned location, pay rent to its owner, draw the appropriate card, or follow a board-space action.
3. **Trade or upgrade:** With available money, negotiate property trades and develop eligible full collections; upgrades should be capped at one per property per turn during early testing.
4. **End turn:** The next player proceeds. Record round advancement after all players finish one turn.

### Corner spaces

- **Andhra Yatra:** Receive ₹2,00,000 on passing or landing on Start, once per lap. Avoid double payment for landing exactly on Start.
- **Traffic Jam:** In the prototype, miss one movement roll and then return to normal play. No real-world enforcement or penalties are implied.
- **Sankranti Fair:** Free rest. In the advanced game, receive one festival token if the bank has any left.
- **Road Block:** Move immediately to the Traffic Jam holding corner without collecting a Start reward for the move.

### Auctions and financial trouble

- If a player declines an unowned property, offer it at a public auction to all players, beginning at ₹10,000. The highest valid bidder pays the bank.
- A player short of cash may sell upgrades back to the bank for half their purchase cost (rounded down to ₹5,000), then sell or trade properties at negotiated prices.
- An unpaid debt after liquidating assets is treated as bankruptcy. In fixed-round games, this player exits and remaining participants finish the game.
- Do not charge rent when the owner is the visiting player.

### First-playtest priorities

- Can a new player understand the rules within **10 minutes**?
- Does the game reliably finish within **45–75 minutes**?
- Are the premium properties exciting without always deciding the winner?
- Are the ₹2 lakh lap salary and upgrade prices creating too much or too little cash?
- Do transport hubs provide interesting decisions rather than dominating the economy?
- Does the regional representation feel balanced and respectful?
- Are festival and event effects fun without feeling unfair?
- Are Telugu names, card copy, and place spellings accurate and legible?

### Production and launch path

1. **Paper prototype:** Board, 30 ownership cards, 40 initial event cards, tokens, and currency.
2. **Run 10–20 playtest sessions:** Include families, new players, and experienced board-game players.
3. **Balance economy:** Revise rent multipliers, starting money, salary, upgrade costs, and event frequency using actual play data.
4. **Illustrate final edition:** Commission original artwork, bilingual card layouts, and accessible iconography.
5. **Validate names and permissions:** Complete trademark checks; avoid reproducing protected Monopoly art, distinctive design, or branding. Consider additional legal review for any commercial launch.
6. **Manufacturing sample:** Check print quality, folding, component durability, packaging, and per-unit cost.
7. **Launch:** Introduce Standard and Deluxe editions only after successful testing.

### Optional expansion ideas

- **Andhra Festival Pack:** Additional Sankranti and seasonal events.
- **Trade Routes Pack:** Deeper port, logistics, and contract strategies.
- **Crafts & Culture Pack:** Additional regional business missions.
- **Digital companion:** Optional scorekeeping, event decks, turn timer, and tutorial—without making a phone mandatory.

## Final creative direction

**Andhra Empire should be more than a board filled with place names.** The most promising version is a colorful, negotiable, and distinctly Telugu commercial world where players combine property portfolios with agriculture, transport, tourism, crafts, and local economic surprises.

Build the **family-friendly core game first**; introduce the more intricate mechanics as optional advanced modules. The goal is a collectible game that feels authentically Andhra while being accessible to anyone encountering it for the first time.
